## ★★★ THE JC4 REFERENCE, DESCRIBED FROM THE ACTUAL WIREFRAMES (2026-08-15 evening)

Ant supplied seven wireframe views of the real Just Cause 4 grapple asset. ArtStation returns 403 to
any automated fetch, so **this written description is the only form of that reference an agent can
reach.** It is written from looking at the images directly.

⚠ LICENCE. This is a DESCRIPTION, which is a fact about a shape. The idea travels. The geometry does
not. Do not import, trace, retopologise over, or copy the reference. Every vertex we ship is
NOX-owned original work.

### The single biggest correction: IT IS A POD, NOT A PLATE

Our attempts have been building a flat plate with parts bolted on. **The JC4 device is a rounded,
chunky POD.** Read that first, because it reframes everything else.

- The silhouette from the side is a **rounded wedge**: tall and full at the REAR, tapering forward
  to a slim nose. Think of a teardrop lying on its side, blunt end backward.
- Body length is roughly **twice its height**. The harpoon adds about another 60 percent of the body
  length again, projecting forward.
- There is real VOLUME. Nothing about it reads as thin or flat.

### The underside is a DEEP CUP, and this is what "conforms to the arm" means

From below, the body is a **large circular concave dish**, a deep saddle that cradles the forearm.
It is not a curved plate laid on top of the arm. The arm sits DOWN INTO it.

★ This is the direct answer to Ant's ruling *"the plate should be rounded so it conforms to the
arm"*, and to her complaint *"also these bits cut into the arm"*. A deep cup cannot cut into the arm
because the arm occupies its interior. A curved plate with parts hanging below it always can.

### The drum: a large concentric disc RECESSED INTO THE TOP REAR

- It sits at the **tallest point of the body**, at the rear, on the TOP surface, not on the flank.
- It is **recessed into** the body, sitting in a circular well, not stuck onto the outside.
- The face reads as **concentric rings** with a **raised central hub**, like a coiled spring or a
  wound spool seen end on.
- **Two rectangular bars cross over the disc face**, a retaining strap or guard. They are chunky and
  they read as mechanical. This is a strong, cheap detail to steal the idea of.
- Disc diameter is roughly **60 to 70 percent of the body height**.

★ This settles Ant's ruling *"have the disk ON the thing instead of to the side"* and her complaint
*"and the disk is disjointed"*. Recessed into a well, with a hub and crossing bars, is the opposite
of disjointed.

### The harpoon projects FORWARD through a FORK of two swept plates

- Two long, tapered, faceted plates sweep forward from the body, one upper and one lower, forming an
  open **fork** or guide channel.
- The harpoon rides in the gap between them. It is a slim shaft with a **knurled or twisted midsection**.
- The head is a **classic barbed arrowhead**: a leaf point with **two swept-back barbs**, integral to
  the tip, not thin spindly spikes.

★ Two rulings land here at once. *"we dont need the thin barbs, thats just the just cause series
having its own flair"* means do not copy the delicate barbs, but the JC4 head is NOT thin, it is a
solid barbed point. And *"it needs to have hooks in the front so it can dig into the earth"* is
exactly what those swept-back barbs do. And *"the hole can be smaller, the point of the projectile
can sit outside of the rope tube"* matches how the head sits proud of the fork.

### Surface treatment

- Broad flat panels separated by **crisp chamfered edges**. Angular, faceted, not smooth-blended.
- **Recessed rectangular slots and vents** on the flanks, long and shallow.
- Small **circular bolt and port details**, used sparingly, at panel junctions.
- Topology is **quad dominant** on the curved shell, triangles only on flat caps and the drum face.

### Proportion notes to steal

| element | relationship |
|---|---|
| body length to body height | about 2 to 1 |
| harpoon projection | about 0.6 of body length, forward of the nose |
| drum diameter | about 0.6 to 0.7 of body height |
| fork plates | run about half the body length forward |
| mass distribution | heavy at the rear, light at the nose |

### ⚠ Ignore the display stand

Every reference view sits on a **round pedestal with two stubby legs**. That is presentation
furniture from the artist. It is NOT part of the device and must not be modelled.

### The checklist for each round

Ask these against our model, every round, and answer each with what you actually measured:

1. Is our body a rounded POD, or still a flat plate with parts on it?
2. Is the underside a DEEP CUP the arm sits into, or a curved lid laid over the arm?
3. Is the drum RECESSED into a well on the top rear, with a hub and crossing bars?
4. Does the harpoon ride in an open FORK of two swept plates?
5. Is the head a solid barbed point, not thin spikes?
6. Is the mass at the REAR, tapering forward?
7. Have we accidentally modelled the pedestal?

### ★★★ SECOND REFERENCE BATCH, TEXTURED VIEWS. THIS CORRECTS THE MOUNTING RULING

Ant supplied a second set of views of the same JC4 asset, textured and shown from the underside and
the rear. They reveal the mounting, which the wireframes hid. This is the most important correction
in the whole brief.

#### IT IS A STRAP-MOUNTED BRACER, NOT A RIGID CLAMP

Under the rigid body there is a **soft harness**:

- A **woven fabric strap**, clearly textile, runs around the forearm. The weave is visible.
- The strap carries **buckles and adjusters**, at least two, with metal keepers and a loose tail end
  hanging past the buckle. It reads as something a person tightens by hand.
- Under the rigid pod sits a **large padded cradle panel**, quilted with shallow rectangular panel
  shapes pressed into it. It is soft-looking, darker than the metal, and it is the part that
  actually touches the arm.
- The arm passes through a large **rounded aperture** formed between the rigid top and the strap
  below. From the rear the opening is almost a full circle.

★ **THIS IS THE ANSWER TO "the plate should be rounded so it conforms to the arm".** The rigid part
does NOT conform. It does not need to. **The SOFT part conforms**, and the rigid pod simply rides on
top of it. That is why the reference has no cutting-into-the-arm problem, and it is why every
attempt to solve this by bending a rigid plate has failed here.

★ **IT ALSO ANSWERS "these bits cut into the arm".** Nothing rigid is asked to hug a curve. The
strap and pad absorb the shape difference.

#### The layered build, top to bottom

1. **Rigid pod on top**, the metal device, with the drum and the fork and the harpoon.
2. **A hard shell skirt** wrapping down the sides, chamfered, still rigid but only descending part
   way. It stops well before the underside of the arm.
3. **A padded cradle** below that, soft, quilted, dark.
4. **A fabric strap with buckles** closing the loop underneath.

#### Further detail the textured views add

- The **drum face** carries **radial tick marks** around its rim, like a gauge or a ratchet ring,
  with a **raised central hub** and a **crossing bar** over it. It reads as a measuring instrument
  as much as a spool. That is a strong NOX-flavoured detail.
- **Three short angled slots** are cut into the flank of each fork plate, evenly spaced. Cheap
  detail, high read.
- The **harpoon head** is confirmed: a leaf point with **two swept-back barbs**, solid, not spindly,
  sitting proud of the fork on a knurled shaft.
- Surfaces carry **grime and wear in the recesses** while the raised faces stay light. That is the
  rustic treatment Ant asked for, and it comes from texture, not geometry.
- The **rear face** is a broad flat cap with the drum dominating it.

#### ⚠ WHAT THIS MEANS FOR OUR MODEL, CONCRETELY

1. Stop trying to make a rigid plate conform to the arm. Build the rigid pod so it rides ABOVE the
   arm, and add a separate soft cradle and strap below it.
2. The strap and the pad are separate objects with their own material. They are not part of the
   metal shell.
3. The device therefore does NOT need to be curved to the arm radius. It needs CLEARANCE above it.
   That is a far easier constraint and it removes the whole penetration problem that consumed
   fourteen versions.
4. ⚠ It also changes what the forearm measurement is FOR. The measurement no longer sets the curve
   of a plate. It sets the SIZE OF THE APERTURE the arm passes through, plus the strap length.

#### Still ignore the presentation furniture

These views sit on a white mannequin arm and a white pedestal. Neither is part of the device.

### ★★★ THIRD REFERENCE BATCH: FINAL MATERIALS. THE PALETTE IS ALREADY NEARLY OURS

Ant supplied a third set, fully textured and coloured. Two of the three things this settles are things
no amount of geometry work would have told us.

#### 1. THE COLOUR SCHEME IS ORANGE ON GUNMETAL, AND THAT IS FICSIT

- Body: a **cool mid grey to gunmetal**, slightly blue leaning, with **white paint scuffs on every
  raised edge and corner**. The wear is on the edges only. Flat faces stay clean.
- Accent: a **saturated safety orange**, used sparingly and deliberately, on
  - a block at the rear cap,
  - a small tab at the front of the fork,
  - and the drum face.
- Soft parts: **near black**, matte, no shine.
- One **rusted bronze bolt** at the drum hub. A single point of true rust, not overall grime.

★ This is close enough to Satisfactory's own palette that the design already reads as in-world. It
also matches NOX. Do not invent a new palette. The designer drops under `20-SOURCES/FPM-brand/`
remain the authority on exact NOX values, and drops are ADDITIVE.

#### 2. THE DRUM IS A GAUGE, NOT JUST A SPOOL

The drum face, seen clearly for the first time:

- A **black dished centre** with fine radial ribs, like the underside of a turbine.
- An **orange spiral or comma sweeping around it**, one thick arc that tapers. It reads as a wound
  line, or as a needle sweep on a dial.
- A **white ring** around that, carrying **fine orange tick marks** all the way round, exactly like
  a measuring scale.
- A **rusted bronze hub bolt** dead centre.
- A **black arm or bar crossing the face** from the hub outward, like a clock hand or a tensioner.

★ THIS IS THE BEST IDEA IN THE WHOLE REFERENCE FOR US. A device whose main visual feature is a
GAUGE is perfect for NOX, whose name means Notice Of eXcess. The drum can read as a meter that tells
the pioneer how much line is out. Take that idea. Do not copy the artwork.

#### 3. THE MOUNT IS A CLOSED CUFF, CONFIRMED

The rear views show a **complete closed loop**, not an open cradle:

- The **rigid metal shell caps only the top and the upper sides**. It stops about halfway down.
- The **entire lower half is soft**: a dark moulded pad with rounded rectangular embossing, like a
  moulded rubber mat, ringed by a **woven strap edge**.
- **Two woven straps** run around the cuff, with buckles and loose tail ends.
- The arm aperture is a **large full circle**, soft rimmed all the way round.

⚠ THE PROPORTION THAT MATTERS: the rigid device is roughly the same length as the cuff, and it sits
ENTIRELY ON TOP. It never wraps below the arm centreline. That is the geometry rule to follow, and
it is what makes the penetration problem disappear.

#### The finishing rules to take

| aspect | rule |
|---|---|
| base | cool grey gunmetal, slightly blue |
| wear | white scuffs on RAISED EDGES only, never on flat faces |
| accent | safety orange, three or four small placements, never a large area |
| soft parts | matte near black, no highlights |
| rust | ONE point of real rust, at the hub. Not general grime. |
| the drum | make it read as an INSTRUMENT: ring, ticks, needle, hub |

⚠ Still ignore the pedestal and the mannequin arm. Neither is part of the device.

### ★ TWO CORRECTIONS TO MY EARLIER WRITE-UP

1. **THERE IS A CYAN EMISSIVE, AND I MISSED IT.** `ref-039` shows a small **cyan or teal glowing
   element** beside the drum hub, and `ref-052` shows a second one on the fork root. I described the
   accent palette as orange only. It is orange PLUS a small cold emissive. That matters: a cold
   indicator against a warm accent is a strong, cheap read, and it is the sort of thing NOX would use
   to show a state rather than to decorate.

2. **THE DRUM RING IS WARM METAL, NOT FLAT ORANGE.** Under the close lighting in `ref-052` the ring
   reads as **gold or aged brass with fine tick marks milled into it**, not as painted orange. My
   earlier note called it a painted safety orange. Under the flatter light of the first batch it does
   look painted. The close-up says otherwise. Treat it as a MATERIAL, not a paint colour.

### One more thing the sample proves about the workflow

`ref-063` being a normal map pass means the original was built **high poly first, then baked**. The
strap weave, the panel bevels and the moulded pad detail are all in the bake, not in the low poly.

⚠ THAT IS NOT OUR ROUTE, and it should not become one by imitation. We are building a low-poly
prototype for a wrist item. Chasing a baked look by adding geometry is how a small prop gets
expensive. Take the FORMS from these images. Do not try to reproduce the surface detail as mesh.
